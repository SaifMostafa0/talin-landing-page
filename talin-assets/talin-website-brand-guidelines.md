# Talin Website Brand Guidelines

Practical web guidance distilled from the approved Talin Brand Identity and Landing Page Content documents.

## Brand essence

Talin is a new Data, AI, Business Intelligence, and Digital Strategy consulting brand launched by CID Consulting.

The brand combines the Sage and Creator archetypes. Every digital expression should feel:

- Trustworthy and confident
- Empowering and future-oriented
- Structured and intelligent
- Human-centric — no one left behind
- Partnership-led and accountable

The website should move visitors from complexity to clarity, then from clarity to measurable business value.

### Color usage

- Dark navy and Talin Blue should establish the primary visual tone.
- Use light blue and the secondary blues to create hierarchy and progression, not visual noise.
- Use white text only where contrast is strong and intentional.
- Do not use the light blue as a logo color.
- Keep color application consistent across headings, body copy, UI controls, backgrounds, and illustrations.
- For accessible text, verify contrast for every text/background pairing. Do not rely on brand color alone to communicate state.

## Gradient and background language

The Talin gradient is an expressive identity element. Use approved blue-spectrum transitions to create depth, movement, energy, and progression while keeping the overall tone refined and professional.

The Talin Star may appear in backgrounds as:

- Subtle curves or orbital lines
- Light transitions and glows
- Cropped geometric forms
- Tonal shifts within a blue gradient
- Focused, expanding shapes that suggest data becoming meaning

The star should remain subtle and geometric. It should never compete with copy, reduce contrast, or be repeated as a decorative pattern on every section.

## Typography

### Raleway — headline typeface

Use Raleway for:

- Hero headlines
- Primary section headings
- Large statements and editorial emphasis

Approved styles include Light, Regular, Italic, and Bold. Headlines should feel spacious, modern, and confident. Use considered tracking for titles and labels; keep body text at natural spacing for readability.

### Urbanist — supporting and body typeface

Use Urbanist for:

- Body copy
- Secondary headings
- Navigation and buttons
- Captions and data labels

Recommended weights from the identity:

- Regular for body text
- Medium or SemiBold for secondary headings
- Medium for captions and data labels

### Web typography rules

- Keep the type hierarchy clear: Raleway for the main voice, Urbanist for information and interface detail.
- Use responsive sizing with a sensible minimum; do not scale type solely from viewport width.
- Maintain readable line lengths and generous vertical rhythm.
- Use uppercase sparingly for navigation, eyebrows, metadata, and labels with increased letter spacing.
- Never substitute an unapproved display font when the Talin fonts are available.
- Provide a robust fallback stack such as `Raleway, Arial, sans-serif` and `Urbanist, Arial, sans-serif`.

## Logo system

### Approved lockups

1. **Primary / full lockup** — symbol + Talin wordmark + “BY CID CONSULTING”. Use when the endorsement is important and there is enough space.
2. **Secondary / reduced lockup** — symbol + Talin wordmark, without the endorsement line. Use in navigation and compact layouts.
3. **Wordmark** — Talin typography only. Use where the symbol is unnecessary or space is limited.
4. **Brand mark** — symbol only. Use for small spaces, compact controls, and decorative focus points.

### Website application

- Use the reduced lockup in a sticky website header when the full lockup would become too small.
- Use the full lockup in the footer or larger brand moments when the endorsement remains legible.
- Preserve the supplied artwork; do not recreate the wordmark with ordinary text.
- Use only approved logo colors. White is appropriate on dark navy, blue, or gradient backgrounds; dark/blue variants are appropriate on light backgrounds.
- Keep the logo optically clear of surrounding text, borders, images, and controls.
- Define clear space using a repeatable unit from the logo, such as the central symbol shape or lowercase “i” height.
- Preserve minimum legibility at every breakpoint and do not let the lockup overflow or become distorted.

### Never

- Stretch, compress, rotate, tilt, or change proportions
- Rearrange or separate the symbol, wordmark, or endorsement line
- Change approved colors or apply gradients directly to the logo
- Add shadows, outlines, glows, or other effects
- Place the logo over busy imagery or inside an unapproved shape
- Violate the exclusion zone or reproduce the logo below a legible size

## Iconography

Talin iconography uses a clean monoline outline style that is modern, approachable, and professional.

- Use simple geometric forms, smooth curves, rounded corners, and consistent stroke weight.
- Keep internal details minimal and purposeful.
- Use Talin Blue as the default icon color.
- Use white icons on dark blue or gradient surfaces when stronger contrast is required.
- Keep icons optically centered with balanced negative space.
- Maintain consistent proportions and visual weight across an icon set.
- Prefer icons that clarify a service, action, or concept; avoid decorative icon overload.

## Layout and composition

- Use a structured grid with generous negative space and strong alignment.
- Let the narrative progress in this order: Human-Led Intelligence → Why Talin Exists → Intelligence in Action → How We Partner → The Minds Behind Talin → Let’s Talk.
- Favor editorial layouts, measured asymmetry, and clear content groupings over dense dashboards.
- Use borders, rules, and tonal blocks to make structure visible without adding ornament.
- Keep important copy and actions within comfortable reading widths.
- Use responsive stacking for service cards, principles, engagement steps, and leadership profiles.
- Keep touch targets large enough for mobile use and preserve visible keyboard focus.

## Interaction and motion

Motion should communicate focus, progression, and clarity.

Good uses:

- Subtle reveal as sections enter the viewport
- Gentle card and button elevation on hover
- Progress lines for the five-step engagement flow
- Controlled glow or tonal movement in abstract background treatments
- Clear active, hover, and focus states

Avoid:

- Rotating or distorting the logo
- Constantly moving backgrounds that compete with reading
- Excessive parallax, bouncing, or decorative animation
- Motion that hides content from users who have reduced-motion preferences

Always support `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` by removing non-essential transitions and reveal delays.

## Content and voice

The tone should be clear, intelligent, human, and grounded in outcomes. Talin speaks as a trusted partner, not as a technology vendor.

Emphasize:

- Real business value
- Measurable and sustainable growth
- Ethical, human-centered transformation
- Independent recommendations
- Partnership and organizational readiness

Avoid:

- Unexplained technical jargon
- Hype without a business outcome
- Vendor-specific language that undermines the tech-agnostic stance
- Claims that are not supported by approved content
